# The Booking Window Playbook: Re-Engaging Guests on Their Timeline, Not Your Email Calendar

*A guide for travel and hospitality marketers*

**By Jessica Vose, Head of GTM at Kana AI · \[DATE\] · 6 min read**

Walk through any hotel marketing team's automation stack and you'll find the same timers. Thank-you email at checkout plus two days. Review request at checkout plus seven. Win-back at 90 days without a booking. Anniversary offer 365 days after the stay.

Every one of those is timed to your calendar. None of them is timed to when the guest books her next trip. The anniversary email celebrates the day she checked out; the trip it's trying to win was booked five months earlier, quite possibly with a competitor.
---

The prize for fixing this is the best margin in hospitality. A guest acquired through your own CRM rebooks at around 33%; a guest acquired through an online travel agency (OTA) rebooks at around 6% (*stayinsiders, 2025–26*). And every direct rebooking skips the 15–25% commission an OTA takes off the top, which can pass 30% with promotional placement. On a property doing $20M in room revenue, a 14-point lift in direct booking share is worth roughly $400–700K a year (*hospitality.today, 2026*).

There's also something a little absurd about the current setup. Hotels run the most sophisticated real-time pricing of any industry: revenue management reprices rooms continuously as demand shifts. Then the marketing team re-engages past guests, the highest-converting audience the property has, on timers that were set once and never questioned. The fastest system in the building and the slowest one share the same P&L.

Meanwhile the pressure to fix it is rising. Loyalty program costs have grown 53.6% since 2022 while room revenue grew 44.1% (*industry analysis, 2026*), and finance teams are asking loyalty and lifecycle marketing to prove incremental revenue, not sends.

The good news is that re-timing post-stay marketing doesn't require new data. It requires reading data you already have. Here's the playbook.

## **Step 1: Find each segment's real rhythm**

Before touching a single email, pull two years of repeat-guest history and measure the gap between *bookings*, booking date to booking date, not stay to stay. Then split it by trip type: the leisure guest who comes every summer, the business traveler on a six-week cycle, the guest who only shows up for one annual event.

The distribution of those gaps is your re-engagement window, and it will look nothing like a single 90-day timer. Most properties have never plotted it. Once you have, every timer in your stack has a number it can be checked against.

## **Step 2: Aim for the booking window, not the stay anniversary**

Here's the mistake buried in the anniversary campaign: it targets the date of the *stay*, but guests decide much earlier. A family that stays every August isn't shopping in August. Depending on the market, they're comparing options in February and booking by April.

So work backwards. Take the typical rebook month for a segment, subtract its typical booking lead time, and that's when your outreach should land: while the decision is still open. An offer that arrives during the stay-anniversary week is simply late. The room she's celebrating the anniversary of was booked months before that email went out.

## **Step 3: Let live intent override the timer**

The calendar math from Step 2 gets you into the right month. Live behavior tells you the exact week. A past guest who suddenly browses dates on your booking engine, opens the app after months of silence, checks her loyalty point balance, or starts clicking emails again is telling you she's planning a trip right now, whatever the timer says.

The reason these signals go unused isn't that they're hidden. It's that they live in four different systems, and fewer than 1 in 4 hotels have their core systems integrated enough to share a single guest profile (*Apptad, 2025–26*). You don't need to solve that overnight. Start with a weekly pull of three signals per past guest: last booking-engine visit, last app or email activity, last loyalty activity. Any guest lighting up two of the three jumps the queue, ahead of every scheduled send.

## **Step 4: Check the rate calendar before you attach an offer**

This is the step almost every lifecycle team skips, and it's where the money quietly leaks. Hospitality's documented failure mode is marketing running discount campaigns on dates the revenue team is trying to hold rate on (*Cloudbeds, 2026*). A 20%-off win-back offer redeemed on a high-demand weekend just hands away margin the pricing team spent all quarter defending. The same offer on a soft midweek in shoulder season is exactly what both teams want.

The fix costs one agenda item: bring the win-back and promo calendar to the weekly revenue meeting. Suppress discounts on dates where demand is already strong, and swap in value-adds instead: an upgrade, late checkout, breakfast. The guest still feels recognized, and the rate holds.

## **Step 5: Measure rebookings, not opens**

An open rate can't tell you whether the email arrived inside anyone's booking window. If the campaign's job is a direct rebooking, measure that: rebook rate by segment, how close each send landed to the guest's eventual booking date, and, on discount campaigns, a holdout group that gets nothing, so you know the offer caused bookings instead of subsidizing ones that were coming anyway.

The benchmark to chase is real. Hotels pairing journey mapping with real-time personalization report booking-conversion lifts of 25–30% (*2026*). But you'll only see that number if conversion is what you're counting.

## **Why nobody's done this already**

None of these steps is conceptually hard, and that's worth sitting with. The reason post-stay marketing still runs on timers isn't laziness. It's that timers are what the tooling makes easy. An email platform will happily fire at checkout plus seven forever; joining booking history, live intent signals, loyalty data, and the rate calendar into one view is a data project most marketing teams can't staff. So the pricing side of the house updates by the hour, and the marketing side syncs with it in a weekly meeting.

## **Booking Window Challenge**

Still skeptical? We're challenging lifecycle and loyalty teams at a variety of properties to do the following:

1. **Plot 100 gaps.** Take your last 100 repeat guests and measure the days between their bookings. Compare the median to your win-back timer. The distance between those two numbers is how far off your calendar is.
2. **Audit last quarter's win-backs.** Of the guests who rebooked after receiving a win-back send, how many booked within 30 days of it? If it's a sliver, your sends are landing outside everyone's booking window.
3. **Cross-check your last discount against demand.** Pull the stay dates where your most recent offer was redeemed and ask your revenue manager how many of those dates they were trying to hold rate on. That's the margin the calendar gave away.

The hotels that win direct share over the next few years won't be the ones with the fullest email calendar. They'll be the ones who noticed the guest has a calendar of her own, and started marketing to that one.
