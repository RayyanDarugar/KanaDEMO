# The Trade Area Playbook: Making the National Ad Budget Work for Every Store

*A guide for QSR and restaurant franchise marketers*

**By Jessica Vose, Head of GTM at Kana AI · \[DATE\] · 6 min read**

Every field marketing manager has sat across from a franchisee holding a comp-sales report (same-store sales versus a year ago), pointing at a number, and asking some version of the same question: we pay into the same ad fund as everyone else, so why does the national campaign work for the store three miles away and not for mine?

There usually isn't a good answer, because the campaign wasn't built to have one. It was planned once, at the DMA level (a media market spanning dozens of stores with wildly different customers, competitors, and traffic patterns), and it runs the same way in all of them until the next planning cycle.
---

The money at stake isn't small. Franchisees put 2–7% of gross sales into national and regional ad funds, on top of a 4–6% royalty most systems already collect. That's real money leaving a franchisee's P&L on the promise that pooled spend outperforms what they could do alone. When the pool clearly isn't working for a given store, the franchisee isn't wrong to be upset. And the industry has given them plenty of reason lately: 42% of operators weren't profitable in 2025, and roughly 30% of visits that year were driven by deals and discounts, the highest rate in 50 years, while total industry traffic still declined. Fifteen of eighteen major QSR brands now have worse guest retention than before the discounting escalated, a sign that the current playbook of throwing national offers at the problem is mostly buying a shrinking pool of deal-hunters, not loyalty.

None of that is a reason to abandon pooled national spend. It's a reason to stop spending it uniformly. Here's how to find where the pool is failing specific stores, and what to do about it before the next franchisee council meeting.

## **Step 1: Find the stores the average is hiding**

Your weekly comp-sales readout almost certainly reports at the DMA or regional level, because that's how the media plan is built. But averages hide exactly what franchisees are complaining about: a DMA showing flat comp sales can contain three stores wildly outperforming and three quietly sinking.

Pull the same comp-sales and comp-traffic numbers you already report weekly, but at the individual store level, and rank every store against its own market's average, not the brand-wide number. The stores sitting furthest from their local peers, in either direction, are where the current spend is doing the wrong job.

## **Step 2: Ask why, before you touch the budget**

This is the step teams skip because it's slower than just moving dollars. A store underperforming its market doesn't automatically need more spend, and a store overperforming isn't automatically wasting its marketing budget. The reasons behind each outlier vary, and each one calls for something different:

- A new competitor opened in the trade area (the roughly three-mile radius most guests draw from) — this store may need sharper local messaging, not more national reach.
- The store is in a weaker trade area for the brand's core guest — no amount of spend fixes a mismatch between the offer and the neighborhood.
- The store is already near capacity at peak day-parts — more traffic-driving spend here just adds wait times, not revenue.
- The store's digital and loyalty mix lags its market — this is often the cheapest fix, because it's an execution gap, not a demand gap.

Sort your outlier stores from Step 1 into these categories before deciding anything about their budget.

## **Step 3: Reallocate to the cluster, not the DMA**

Once you know why stores are diverging, group them into clusters that share a reason, not a zip code. A cluster of stores facing new competitive pressure needs a different response than a cluster that's simply under-digital. Then shift spend and creative by cluster: sharper local offers where a competitor just moved in, retention-focused push and email where digital mix is lagging, and day-part-specific timing where the store is already busy at lunch but empty at dinner.

This is the part a quarterly DMA plan can't do. The plan was locked months ago, split evenly across a market that never divides evenly.

## **Step 4: Bring the franchisee into the reallocation, not just the readout**

The franchisee council meeting is usually where corporate defends spend that already happened. Flip it: show up with the cluster your franchisee's store belongs to, why it landed there, and what's changing because of it. A franchisee who sees their store diagnosed specifically, rather than folded into a regional average, stops asking why the pool ignores them, because it visibly didn't.

## **Step 5: Report lift by cluster, not blended comp sales**

The current ad-fund ROI report almost always blends results back into one regional number, which is precisely the average that hid the problem in Step 1. Report the outcome the way you diagnosed it: did the competitive-pressure cluster's comp traffic move after the local offer, did the under-digital cluster's app and loyalty sign-ups climb. A blended number can stay flat while three of your five clusters genuinely improved.

## **Why this still isn't standard practice**

Nothing above requires data you don't already collect. Every restaurant chain already runs weekly comp-sales reports, quarterly ad-fund reviews, and franchisee council meetings. What's missing is the connective step: pulling those reports down to the store level, sorting the outliers by cause, and reallocating before the next quarterly cycle locks in. Most field marketing teams are stretched across dozens or hundreds of stores with a planning cadence, not a monitoring one, so the DMA average is what gets acted on because it's what's already built.

## **Trade Area Challenge**

Still skeptical? We're challenging field and franchise marketing teams at a variety of brands to do the following:

1. **Pull one DMA's store-level comp sales.** Rank every store against its own market average. Count how many sit meaningfully above or below the pack. That count is how much your DMA-level plan is currently averaging away.
2. **Sort your five biggest outliers by cause.** For each one, ask whether it's competitive pressure, trade-area mismatch, capacity, or a digital/loyalty gap. If you can't answer without guessing, that's the diagnosis step your current reporting skips.
3. **Re-run your last ad-fund ROI report by cluster instead of by region.** If the regional number stayed flat while individual clusters moved in opposite directions, that's the story your franchisee council never saw.

The chains that hold onto franchisee trust over the next few years won't be the ones spending the most from the pool. They'll be the ones who can finally show each operator that the pool is working for their store specifically, not just for the average.
