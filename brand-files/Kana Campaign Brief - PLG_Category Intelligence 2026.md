| WHAT: Key Pain Points |
| :---- |

Research across category management thought leaders, trade publications, and analyst content surfaced six primary pain points that the Kana Category Intelligence Hub directly addresses. These pain points should anchor all messaging.

### **Pain Point 1: Intelligence Is Scattered and Manual**

| Category managers spend 30–40% of their time hunting across analyst reports, trade publications, social channels, and internal databases — then manually synthesizing findings into slides. By the time the research is assembled, it’s often stale and disconnected from what’s actually happening in their own sales data. | Kana answer: Category Intelligence Hub continuously ingests external signals and synthesizes them automatically, with source citations, on a weekly cadence. |
| :---- | :---- |

### **Pain Point 2: POS Data Tells You What Happened, Not What’s Coming**

| Point-of-sale data shows what sold last week. It doesn’t explain why the category is moving, where it’s going, or what signals to watch. Teams are making forward-looking decisions (buyer meetings, innovation bets, brand planning) on backward-looking data. | Kana answer: Spike detection in search trends identifies category keyword movements 6–10 weeks before they appear in POS data — giving teams a genuine early-mover window. |
| :---- | :---- |

### **Pain Point 3: No Connection Between External Signals and Internal Scan Data**

| Even teams that do invest in external intelligence (analyst reports, trade pub subscriptions) have no way to automatically cross-reference those signals against their own sales data. So they can’t tell whether an analyst’s trend claim is confirmed, contradicted, or inconclusive in their own numbers. | Kana answer: Every external signal is automatically joined to proprietary scan data and flagged as confirmed, contradicted, or inconclusive — so the strategic response is obvious, not guesswork. |
| :---- | :---- |

### **Pain Point 4: Entering Buyer Meetings Flat-Footed**

| Category teams enter line reviews, joint business planning (JBP), and innovation meetings without a current, defensible view of their categories. Buyer questions like “Why is private label taking share?” or “What’s driving the premium tier?” often can’t be answered on the spot — requiring costly follow-ups that erode credibility. | Kana answer: Category managers can ask plain-English questions and get grounded, source-cited answers in seconds — so they walk into every meeting armed with a current, defensible category view. |
| :---- | :---- |

### **Pain Point 5: Analyst Contradictions Go Undetected**

| When multiple analyst sources disagree on a trend’s direction, category teams often don’t realize it. They may go to market on a single analyst’s claim that is contradicted by three others — or by their own sales data. | Kana answer: The weight-of-evidence feature clusters signals across independent sources and surfaces how many corroborate vs. contradict any claim — so teams distinguish a confirmed industry theme from a single opinion. |
| :---- | :---- |

### **Pain Point 6: Data Overload Without Clarity or Action**

| Most category teams don’t suffer from a lack of data — they suffer from too much data with insufficient synthesis. Dashboards show what moved; they don’t explain why or what to do. The bottleneck is not access to information, it’s translation from data to decision. | Kana answer: Conversational AI interface lets category managers ask questions the way they think and get structured, cited answers — no SQL, no analyst queue, no data engineering involvement. |
| :---- | :---- |

The PLG model means the product itself drives acquisition. Channels should focus on getting qualified category managers into a trial and letting the product convert them. The strategy prioritizes owned and targeted paid channels over broad awareness.

### **LinkedIn — Paid \+ Organic (High Priority)**

* **Why:** Category managers are highly active on LinkedIn; it offers precise title \+ company size \+ industry targeting.

* **Paid:** Sponsored content targeting Category Manager, Senior Category Manager, Director of Category Management, Director of Shopper Insights, VP Category Management — at companies 1,000+ employees in CPG, retail, QSR, travel, hospitality. Budget: 35% of paid spend.

* **Organic:** Vivek’s and Kana’s profiles post demo video clips, trend examples, and behind-the-data insights 3x/week. Use the “See Demand Before It Moves” series.

* **Ad formats:** Video ads using the three Vivek demo videos; document ads for white paper; single-image lead gen ads with trial CTA.

### **Email Nurture (High Priority)**

* **Why:** Direct access to trial sign-ups, warm leads, and in-market contacts. Highest conversion channel for PLG products.

* **Sequence 1**: 5-email onboarding / trial nurture (sent to trial sign-ups; see Content Assets below).

* **Sequence 2**: 6-email cold outreach sequence to target-title list at enterprise accounts (sourced from ZoomInfo / Apollo).

### **Trade Publications — Earned \+ Sponsored Content**

* CPG: Supermarket News, Progressive Grocer, Food Business News, The Food Institute

* QSR: Nation’s Restaurant News, QSR Magazine, FSR Magazine

* Travel / Hospitality: Skift, Hotels Magazine, HotelManagement.net

* Retail: Retail Dive, Chain Store Age, RetailWire

* Approach: Pitch one guest column per publication (white paper-derived, thought leadership angle). Negotiate sponsored content placement for the category intelligence gap assessment tool. Budget: 15% of paid spend.

### **SEO / Content (Medium Priority, Long-Term Compounding)**

* **Target keywords:** “category intelligence platform,” “category manager tools,” “category management software,” “early demand signals CPG,” “buyer meeting preparation CPG,” “category data for QSR”

* **Blog posts:** 2x/month targeting informational and problem-aware keywords. Establish Kana as the destination for category intelligence content.

* **Expected ramp:** SEO traffic meaningful after 8–12 weeks; prioritize for sustained growth beyond the 12-week sprint.
